### Owner: "what else could be worked on?" then "yeah churn on the f-strings work now"

Offered only CPU work that stays off the GPU, Maya, Blender and the orchestrator while the owner plays. The f-string
pass was the pick. A scan by AST (not grep, since many calls span lines) found 71 lazy `%`-format log calls in 20
files; a converter rewrote 67 and left 4 to hand (starred arguments, a `%s` with a tuple). Fourteen came out over the
line limit because adjacent string literals had merged into one: those were wrapped, and the seven with awkward
expressions got a named local first (a `join` inside an f-string reads badly). The whole diff was read for
placeholder-to-argument order; `%g`, `%.2f`, `%r` mapped to `:g`, `:.2f`, `!r`.

Ruff has no rule for the policy: its logging rules flag the f-string form, never the lazy form with arguments. So
`tools/check_log_format.py` is an AST check with a `# lazy-log:` marker for a measured hot path, a CI step and a test.
Proved it can produce the other answer: run on the pre-change tree it finds all 71.
