### Owner: "go - best recommendations"; the legacy ball; Marmoset one-shot

The recommendation I took was the shader-ball variant, and the first step was to look at what the owner remembered. I
opened every legacy scene in a headless Maya (one I started, not the orchestrator's): `shaderBall.ma` and the pbr
scenes are the mesh HogShade already uses (34,623 vertices, one UV set, u from -1 to 1.66, 5,060 shells),
`shaderBall_specular.ma` is three of them, and the v2.0 scene splits the ball into nine parts, still one UV set each.
The owner's other memory was right: the v2.0 parts carry two colour sets (`colorSet0`, `colorSet1`, the vertex colour
and vertex AO the shell reads). So there was no clean ball to bring in, and the answer to "a clean unwrap" is the thing
the owner had asked for the day before: a quad sphere. A cube mapped onto a sphere, every face one 0 to 1 tile,
generated in numpy (`hogshade.testdata.quad_sphere`), radius 1 at the origin so it frames like the normalised ball,
with an analytic inverse (the unit direction gives the face and the UV) and an analytic tangent. The host's
MikkTSpace tangents from its UVs match the analytic ones to under half a degree with no vertex split and every face
right-handed. Because the sphere is analytic, the host probe no longer needs a vertex stand-in: a ray through each
pixel hits the sphere, gives the face and UV, and the texel is a lookup. Roughness, metalness, AO and the colour
patches agree on 100 percent of the flat pixels (1,100 to 9,000 each), and both normal channels on 100 percent of the
leaning pixels as authored (1,522 green, 6,593 red) and 0 flipped. That answered the review's open question from
yesterday: the shader ball's 66 percent green residual was the ball's interpolated frames and the vertex proxy, not
the host.
The orchestrator came up while this was being written. The synthetic set's Maya capture ran on the resident GUI worker
in one submit: all nine textures decoded, and the metalness checker reads in Maya's view 7 exactly as it does in the
wgpu host. Maya still draws its stock polar sphere; the quad sphere in the Maya check is on the board.
One slip worth the ledger's attention but not an entry: a patch script aborted halfway on a stale expectation and the
`texture_matrix` run that followed had already overwritten the committed `matrix.json` legend with a two-set one; git
restored it and the tool now writes a non-default mesh's legend under its own directory.
